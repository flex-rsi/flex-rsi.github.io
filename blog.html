Tech reportNovember 2026Flex-RSI Team

Can an agent improve the way it works?

Agents are usually tested inside a fixed harness: the prompts, tools and control loop are set by someone else, and only the answer is scored. In Flex-RSI, the agent may rewrite that harness as it goes. We gave 18 frontier models the same starting harness, a fixed budget and 24 vision and robotics tasks, and watched what they built.

In short

  1. 01Open-ended tasks leave more room. Where the agent builds its own solution, self-improvement adds the most and the models pull apart; where the solution is one answer, they end about as far apart as they began.
  2. 02The granularity of feedback matters. With a score on a scale, open-weight models improve too; with only success or failure, they barely move while closed models still climb.
  3. 03Strict checks are the hardest. Outputs that look right often fail checks of physical stability or exact correctness, and that is where open-weight models fall furthest behind.
  4. 04Editing the harness helps. Letting the improver edit the harness raises the test score in six of seven pairs of otherwise identical runs.

The setup

Same start, same budget, different agents

Each task gives the agent a starter solution, compute, a budget and three disjoint data splits. The development split, with labels, is the agent's to experiment on. The validation split is hidden and decides which candidates are kept. The test split is hidden too and is used once, after the run, to score the final solution.

The harness has two parts that take turns. The executor works on the solution: it edits code, runs experiments and evaluates on the development split. The improver reads the round's transcript and scores and edits the harness itself, including the executor's prompts, tools and workflow, its own prompts, and the rule that decides when a candidate is good enough. Every run of every model starts from the same general-purpose harness, and on a given task every model has the same limits on time, GPU hours, tokens and rounds.

Who does best

Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon lead

Metrics differ across tasks, so we compare models by rank: on each task a model scores the share of the other models it beats, from 0 (last) to 100 (first). A model's score is the mean over the five areas. Claude Opus 5.5 is first with 91.6; it ran on all 20 tasks and has the best test score on 11. Gemini 4 Argon follows with 89.0 over its nine tasks. The best open-weight model, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, places fifth.

Finding 01

Open-ended tasks leave more room

On seven tasks the solution is a single answer to each question, and the starter gets it by passing the question to the model. These starters score high from the start (78.2 at the median) and depend on the model, so the models are already 27.5 points apart before round one. They end 25.0 points apart: about where they began.

On the other thirteen, the agent has to build the solution: a program, a robot policy, a processing pipeline. Every model starts from the same program, so they start level, and then they separate. On the nine of these scored on a scale, the models are 0.9 points apart at the start and 38.8 at the end.

Finding 02

The granularity of feedback matters

A candidate is kept only if it raises the validation score. When the evaluator scores each episode on a scale, a change that improves part of an episode, a better grasp or a closer approach, is rewarded. When it records only success or failure, a change is rewarded only once it turns a failed episode into a successful one. Everything before that looks like zero.

Closed models can cross that gap; open-weight models mostly cannot. On RoboCasa, whose evaluator records only success, the median open-weight run gains 5.6 points and the median closed run 51.2. On 3D Tracking and Active Search, scored on a scale, open-weight runs gain about as much as closed ones.

Finding 03

Looking right is not being right

The gap between the best closed and the best open-weight model is largest where the evaluator accepts an output only if it passes a strict check: the robot task succeeds in simulation, the output grid is exactly right, the level is cleared, the reconstructed scene is physically and geometrically consistent. It is smallest where a judge or a tolerance does the scoring.

Finding 04

Does editing the harness help?

To isolate the improver, we ran pairs of runs that are identical (task version, budget, protocol and seed) except for one thing: in one, the improver never runs and the starting harness stays frozen, so only the executor works on the solution. The test score with the improver is higher in six of the seven pairs, by 2.0 to 40.0 points. In the seventh, Claude Opus 5.5 reaches 80.0 both ways.

The difference is largest where the unchanged harness makes little progress. DeepSeek-V4.1 Flash stays at 0.0 on Open Drawer without the improver and reaches 40.0 with it; Qwen3.8-Flash-Next goes from 21.0 to 59.3 on Active Search.

Can a weaker model borrow a stronger model's harness? Only partly. DeepSeek-V4.1 Flash, started from Gemini 4 Argon's final executor on Open Drawer, reaches 20.0 if the executor stays frozen and 60.0 if Argon's final improver keeps editing it, against 40.0 when it improves its own harness from scratch. On Turn On Sink Faucet, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with Claude Opus 5.5's harness reaches 0.0 either way. A borrowed harness helped on one of two tasks, and only when the improver came with the executor.

When and at what cost

Gains come early; tokens do not buy them

Most of the gain arrives in the first few rounds: the median improving run reaches 90% of its gain by round 3 in Perception and Reasoning and by round 4 in Creation. Action and Reconstruction, the two areas with the weakest starters, take until rounds 6 and 8. There the agent first has to build something that works before it can refine it.

Takeaways

What this means

Self-improvement is not one number. It depends on what the task leaves the agent to build, on how finely the evaluator reports progress, and on how strictly the output is checked. On tasks with a single answer, the starter already does most of the work, and the ranking mostly reflects the base model. On tasks where the agent builds the solution, models that start from the same program end far apart, and the harness the agent writes for itself is part of why.

Flex-RSI is an evolving benchmark. The leaderboard is updated as new runs finish, and new tasks, evaluators and results are welcome.
